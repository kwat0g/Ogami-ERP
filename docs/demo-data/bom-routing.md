# BOM and routing master data

`bom-routing-catalog` creates the current active bill of materials and production routing for each of the 16 synthetic finished products. It depends on `production-assets`, which supplies all product-linked molds and compatible injection machines. It creates master definitions only; no work orders, material issues, receipts, or production outputs are created here.

## Seeded coverage

| Record | Count | Current service |
|---|---:|---|
| Active BOM versions | 16 | `BomService::create` |
| BOM component lines | 44 | Created by `BomService` and costed by `BomCostingService` |
| Active product routings | 16 | `ProductionRoutingService::create` |
| Routing operations | 48 | Created by `ProductionRoutingService` |

Every product has one active BOM with a resin component, a protective-bag allocation, a colorant line where its synthetic finish is black, and a brass insert on `CH-001`. Every routing has injection molding, gate trimming/edge finishing, and final inspection/packing. The injection operation links to a mold assigned to that product and to a currently usable compatible press. Injection and final inspection/packing operations are marked `qc_required`.

The `small`, `demo`, and `full` scales all retain the 16-product routing coverage. Small mode uses one mold per product, four representative presses, and three auxiliary assets so E2E baselines can exercise all cataloged finished goods. Demo and full modes use the larger 60- and 96-mold asset profiles.

## Costing behavior

BOM quantities are synthetic per finished piece. The recipes use illustrative resin mass, 2.5% resin waste, masterbatch at 2% of resin mass on black parts, one protective bag per 50 parts, and one brass insert per `CH-001`. Resin/component costs come from the inventory items' current standard costs. The seed publishes routings before BOMs so the application's normal BOM costing includes operation conversion costs and records `standard_cost+routing` as the basis. Setup time is allocated across a 5,000-piece cost batch.

Routing labor, machine, and overhead rates are synthetic manufacturing-cost inputs. Injection uses an illustrative labor rate of PHP 160/hour, overhead of PHP 250/hour, and press rate of PHP 450/hour plus PHP 1.50 per tonnage unit/hour. Finishing and packing rates are also illustrative and do not derive from employee payroll. They are not statutory rates, approved shop rates, quotes, or actual plant costs.

Current product setup deliberately leaves selling prices unset and portal visibility restricted. The application may emit cost-update and pricing-review notifications for the resulting open product cost/price review when eligible role accounts exist. These are live open work generated by the application's pricing flow; no email is enabled by this layer. Tests run without demo recipients and report the app's expected "no eligible recipients" warning.

An idempotent rerun compares the active routing operations and BOM lines with the planned definition. It reuses a matching version and asks the app to refresh a stale cost snapshot. If the planned definition changes, the routing and BOM services publish a new version rather than rewriting the historical definition.

The current inventory reference set did not contain PA6 GF30 for synthetic product `HS-001`. This unit adds `RM-009` through the inventory item service so the BOM matches the product's stated resin family. Its standard cost and planning values remain demo estimates. All resin quantities, colorant allocations, bag usage, insert use, cycle times, and conversion rates are documented assumptions; see F-018 in [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md).
